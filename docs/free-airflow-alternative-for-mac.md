Airflow is a well-regarded paid app for casting video from a computer to a TV. If you'd rather not pay, or you want something open source, [Driftcast](https://github.com/leechsoftware/driftcast/releases/latest) does the same job on a Mac for free. It also covers what people look for in Beamer, Elmedia Player, JustStream and similar apps.

## What Driftcast does

- Plays to **Chromecast, Apple TV and AirPlay 2 TVs**, and on the Mac itself
- Opens **MKV, AVI, WMV, TS, VOB, MOV, MP4** and many more
- **Passes through** HEVC, HDR, Dolby Vision and Dolby Atmos where the device supports them
- **Converts only what the device can't play**, on the GPU, while you watch
- Handles **subtitles** in any text encoding, reads Blu-ray and DVD picture subtitles into text, and finds more online
- Shows **posters, plot, cast and ratings**, and keeps **playlists** with resume and watched marks
- Costs nothing, with **no trial, account, ads or tracking**, and is open source under the GPL

## Where a paid app may still suit you better

- **You use Windows.** Driftcast is Mac only.
- **You rely on Chromecast and need something proven.** Driftcast hasn't been tried on a real Chromecast yet, while established apps have years of use behind them. AirPlay has been tested on a Samsung TV.
- **You need a phone remote** or spatial audio, which Driftcast doesn't have yet.

## Other free options

- **VLC** is free and can cast to Chromecast, though users have long reported trouble with subtitles.
- **Chrome** can cast a tab or an MP4, but it mirrors what's on screen for most other files.
- **Plex and Jellyfin** are good if you want a full media server, which is more setup than playing one file.
